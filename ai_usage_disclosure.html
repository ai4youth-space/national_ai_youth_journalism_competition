AI Usage Disclosure

This page explains how AI tools were used in the reporting, music experiments, revision process, and webpage presentation for the article.

Overview

This article investigates whether GPT-6 Astra's performance on a Bach-style counterpoint benchmark should be understood as a true musical breakthrough, a strong technical result, or something more complicated. Because the article itself is about AI and music, AI tools were also used as part of the reporting and experimentation process.

AI was used as an assistant for research organization, experiment design, drafting support, and webpage production. The article's central question, personal interpretation, music-making decisions, and final editorial choices remained human-directed.

Research Support

AI tools were used to help find online material that supported, questioned, or complicated the article's main claims. This included public posts, comments, recaps, product announcements, and discussion around the Bach Benchmark, GPT-6 Astra, AI music generation, and reactions from musicians or technologists.

The purpose of this step was not to treat AI search results as final evidence. Instead, AI helped collect leads and links that could be checked later. Any factual claim based on a post, article, or comment should still be verified against the original source before submission.

Source Categorization

AI was used to summarize and group the gathered material into categories. These categories helped shape the article's structure and made it easier to compare different viewpoints.

  • Supportive reactions that treated Astra's chorale as a meaningful technical achievement.
  • Skeptical reactions arguing that Bach-style chorale generation is an old benchmark, not a new kind of creativity.
  • Critical reactions focused on whether the result sounded emotionally flat or lacked expressive intent.
  • Contextual notes about Bach chorales, voice-leading rules, and the limits of benchmark-based evaluation.
  • Practical examples of AI working inside music-production environments rather than only generating finished audio.

Music Experiments

AI was also the main tool used in the creative experiments described in the article. GPT-6 Astra was used to generate and revise instrumental music ideas, including pieces written in ABC notation. The prompts, revision requests, listening choices, and decisions about what worked or failed were directed by the author.

SUNO was used as an audio-generation tool to help turn arrangement ideas into listenable audio. In the original-song experiment, the author had already written the lyrics and melody. AI was used to help explore arrangement, production, and sound, not to replace the original musical idea.

Revision Support

AI was used to suggest revisions based on article drafts. This included help with organization, transitions, clarity, headline/dek alternatives, and identifying places where the argument needed more nuance or verification.

Revision suggestions were treated as suggestions. The final decisions about wording, structure, emphasis, and what claims to include were made by the author.

Webpage and Visual Presentation

AI tools were used to help design and build the webpage that presents the article. The abstract hero artwork was also AI-generated and then selected for its fit with the story's themes: Bach-style notation, technical evaluation, and machine-learning interpretation.

This disclosure page is part of the article's transparency record and can be updated as media links, source checks, and competition submission materials are finalized.
